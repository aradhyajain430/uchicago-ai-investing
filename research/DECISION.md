# Investment decision checkpoint

The initial LONG hypothesis did not pass the model. The working recommendation is SHORT Fabrinet, with a 3–12 month horizon and a 12-month DCF target rounded to $275 versus the September 17, 2026 close of $380.92.

This is an expectation-and-cash-conversion thesis. It does not require a collapse in AI infrastructure demand. The base forecast permits about 29% revenue growth in FY2027, then moderates growth by end market. Cash tax expense, capital spending and working capital are explicit.

## What changed the initial view

1. The next-quarter non-GAAP EPS guidance excludes $0.40 of Pillar Two tax expense. This is a disclosed charge, not evidence of accounting misconduct or a newly discovered fact. The thesis concerns its economic weight and persistence.
2. FY2026 cash capex was $252.5m, while capex including amounts accrued was $298.9m. The filing expects FY2027 capex to increase; these definitions must not be mixed.
3. The FY2026 $57.4m Pillar Two current tax accrual had not all passed through cash taxes. The model includes an explicit settlement assumption, separately from tax on future operating earnings.
4. A $75m term loan was entered into after the fiscal year end. The valuation bridge reflects both the proceeds and debt while retaining forecast capex.
5. An earnings-multiple cross-check supports a higher value than the DCF. That disagreement and the $510 bull DCF are retained as risks rather than hidden.

## Conditional assumptions, not facts

- WACC 10%, terminal growth 3%, terminal ROIC 20% in the base case.
- Forecasts and the 20% / 60% / 20% scenario weights are analyst judgments.
- First-year cash flow is prorated from the latest reported fiscal year end to September 18; no current-quarter cash balance is claimed as reported.
- The forecast uses June 30 year-end dates for transparent date calculations; actual fiscal dates vary.
- Reference borrow cost is an illustrative 3% annually. Availability and actual fees are not verified.
- A 50% haircut to nonmarketable equity holdings and a 2%-of-revenue operating cash reserve are analyst assumptions.
