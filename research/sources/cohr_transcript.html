INDUSTRY GLOSSARY

  • Indium Phosphide (InP): A semiconductor material used in high-speed and high-power optoelectronics, critical for manufacturing lasers and transceivers.
  • 800G/1.6T: High-bandwidth optical transceiver standards capable of transmitting 800 gigabits and 1.6 terabits per second, respectively.
  • OCS (Optical Circuit Switching): A technology that uses mirrors to steer light beams directly between fiber-optic cables, reducing power and latency.
  • CPO (Co-packaged Optics): An integration technology that brings optical components close to processing units to improve energy efficiency and bandwidth.
  • NPO (Near-packaged Optics): An intermediate architectural step toward co-packaging where optical modules are mounted on the same motherboard as the processor.
  • XPU: A general term for processing units such as CPUs, GPUs, or TPUs used in data center computing.
  • Multi-rail: A networking architecture that uses multiple parallel links between data centers to handle massive AI workloads.
  • LTA (Long-term Agreement): A contract between a supplier and customer defining volume commitments, pricing, and supply guarantees over several years.
  • EML (Electro-absorption Modulated Laser): A high-performance laser diode used for long-reach and high-speed optical communications.
  • ZR/ZR+: Standards for coherent optical transceivers that enable high-bandwidth data transmission over distances up to 120 kilometers.
